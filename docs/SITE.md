# How the site works

A page-by-page description of what https://budget.nazwel.net shows and why, for the people who maintain it: which figure each year shows, how totals and notes are chosen, which file each part reads, and the address parameters. The plain-language guide for visitors is the [README](../README.md). For how the PDFs become data, see [PIPELINE.md](PIPELINE.md); to rebuild everything from a fresh clone, [REBUILD.md](REBUILD.md); to find and change a setting, label or color, [REFERENCE.md](REFERENCE.md).

## 1. How the data is presented

### One kind of figure per year, plus the year's budget
Each budget book prints several years side by side. The site shows **one main kind of figure per year**, so years are never mixed up (this is what the multi-year charts and tables draw):

| Year | Figure shown | Where it comes from |
|---|---|---|
| Historic years (2020–2025) | **Actual** | The book published two years later |
| Prior budget year (2026) | **Projected** (year-end estimate) | The newest book. The revised projection is used when one is printed. |
| New budget year (2027) | **Proposed** and **Revised** | The newest book. You can switch between the two. Neither is an adopted budget. |
| Later years (2028–2029) | **Projection** | The city's `2028-2029-Budget-Projections.pdf`, made with the 2027 budget. Shown only at the end of the multi-year charts and tables; never a year you can pick. |

**Projections** are the city's own estimates for the two years after the newest budget. They extend **Totals by Year** (home, every fund, department and bureau pages) and each Staff **Salaries and wages** row's history (and are a Compare with choice in the Staff ledgers): a gray dotted line with hollow points continuing from the 2027 Revised point, over a lightly shaded band labelled "Projection", with muted year labels, and muted italic rows or columns marked "Projection" in the tables. A note under the chart says they are projections, gives the city's assumptions (no water, sewer, stormwater or trash rate increases; 3% salary increases; and the rest, behind "The city's assumptions", linked to the assumptions page), the fund's printed surplus or deficit when one fund is in view, and, for the General Fund, that the 2027 income counts money drawn from savings while the projections count none. The year picker, the headline total, the change sentence and What changed stay on the newest year. The projections book has no Fund Balance Projection page, so the Reserve Fund ends at 2027.

**The year's budget (Amended, else Adopted)** (owner's decision, 2026-11-27): each year also has **one** budget figure that can be picked in **Budget year** and **Compare with**: the **Amended** budget where the next year's book prints an amended column for it, otherwise the **Adopted** budget, never both. It comes from the book a year later (the same newest-book rule as the other figures), is decided from the data (`build_dataset.py` `BUDGET_MEASURES`), and links to its PDF page like every figure. Today every year from 2021 to 2026 has an amended column, so all of them show as Amended ("2026 Amended" from the 2027 book); a year whose book printed only an adopted column would show "Adopted". It is never drawn on Totals by Year, Over time or the Staff salaries history, which keep one figure per year. Choosing it changes the headline, deck, status stamp ("2026: Amended budget") and every table on the page, as any other figure does.

Year-to-date columns and an older book's own-year proposal are **not shown**. They stay in the raw extractions in `data/processed/`. When a new book arrives, the year labels move forward automatically: the new year becomes Proposed/Revised, the year before becomes Projected and gains its Amended (or Adopted) budget, and older years become Actual.

### Every amount links to its source page
Clicking a dollar amount opens the budget-book PDF **at the page it came from**. This works for totals, categories and line items. Page numbers are not printed on screen, because the link is the amount itself.

### City and County
The two tabs at the top of the left rail (**City**, **County**) switch between the City of Lancaster and the County of Lancaster. County pages live at `#/county/...`; a plain `#/...` address is still the City, so every existing link works. A bare address opens the entity the visitor last chose (kept in `localStorage`). `web/config.json` has an `entities` map (name, wording, default fund, fund names, data and PDF paths); the County's data is `web/data/county/` and its books are served from `pdfs/county/` (`Budget PDFs - City/county` is a symlink to `Budget PDFs - County`, so the publish workflow's `cp -RL` copies them).

The County view shows the same pages with County figures, all labelled **Budget** (the newest year, 2026, was adopted Dec 24, 2025 by Resolution 79 of 2025), plus:
- the fund letters with their names (Liquid Fuels, Drug & Alcohol, Early Intervention, Domestic Relations, Aging, Behavioral Health, Developmental Services, Children & Youth);
- departments grouped as the County's book groups them (General Government, Courts and Row Offices, Corrections, Human Services), each with its departments and line items;
- the deck sentence "General Fund spending up $15.8M (8.5%) from the 2025 Budget, spending $9.0M more than it expects to collect", with a note that the year's budget resolution covers the gap from the General Fund's balance (linked to the General Fund Balance page);
- **Budget and Actual**: audited 2023–2025 Actual from the County's annual financial reports beside the Budget, only at fund totals (income and spending), plus General Fund income by source and spending by function as their own small tables. No variance is shown, and the report's own "original budget" column is never used;
- **General Fund Balance** (last in the Fund list, and in the header, as the City's Reserve Fund): two measures side by side, never combined. **Budget**, from the budget resolution adopted with each year's budget (Res 91 of 2023, Res 83 of 2024, Res 79 of 2025): the balance at the start of the year, receipts, expenditures, the planned draw (calculated: $5.4M in 2025, $9.0M in 2026; 2024 planned a $100,000 addition) and the balance remaining. **Actual**: the audited balance at December 31 for 2023–2025 and its unassigned part, from the annual financial reports. A grouped chart, a per-year table and a quiet note under each Budget year give the audited balance for the same date; the resolution balances are budget estimates and do not tie (Res 83 dates $69,155,866 12/31/2024, but it is the audited 12/31/2023 balance; logged in the known issues). Every figure links to its PDF page;
- **The 2026 Budget in Context** (overview, below Budget and Actual): the 10% tax increase and the tax rate (3.201 mills, from 2.911), the taxable assessed value, the bond rating (Aa2), the funded staffing positions (1,929), the Capital Improvement Fund ($10,109,623 for 2026: $4,209,623 carried over from 2025 and $5,900,000 of new projects, counted apart from the operating budget), the agency grants (five from the General Fund, $2,560,716; five from the County's Act 13 funds, $700,000; the RRTA/SCTA transit line of $542,008 is separate) and a small chart of the tax rate from 2017 to 2026, with a note that the 2018 drop came with a countywide reassessment. They come from `data/county/context_facts.csv`; each figure links to the page of the resolution, budget book or audited report that prints it. Only the staffing count rests on the County's budget presentation alone, and says so. None of the presentation's balances or revenue and spending figures is used;
- on About, the General Fund balance section (both measures, the resolutions in use, and the audited balances of every fund group);
- no Staff section (the County prints no staffing schedule);
- departments headed by an elected official (the Commissioners and nine row offices) are labelled "Elected office" on their page and in the department lists, and the two that serve elected judges (Judicial Operations, Magisterial District Court) "Elected judges". `data/county/elected_offices.csv` holds the list with the page that says so, and About explains how the County is run. The book's groups and printed totals are unchanged.

### What the site shows
- **Home (all departments):**
  - a plain opening sentence saying whether the total is up or down against the comparison, by how much, and, for the General Fund, how much is **drawn from the reserve** (the "Fund Balance Reserve" revenue lines, object `499000`): "Up $5.0M (6.0%) from 2026 Projected, with $4.7M drawn from savings ($1.9M of it from dedicated data center revenue). See the Reserve Fund". Where the Fund Balance Projection page prints a "Committed data center revenue" adjustment and it plus the operating deficit equals the draw (2027 Revised: $4,735,088, of it $1,934,343 data center; 2026 Projected: $6,315,053, of it $4,162,525), the total comes first and the data center part follows in parentheses, both linked to that page; otherwise (2027 Proposed, whose page is $475,000 off) the total stands alone, linked to its revenue page. The same split is used wherever the draw is described: the What changed sentence, tile and lists, the "Fund Balance Reserve" line labels and the Reserve Fund page's tie note; `check_dataset.py` checks both parts against the fund balance CSV and that they add up to the draw. The draw is summed from the data for each figure (2027 Revised $4,735,088; 2027 Proposed $4,268,753; 2026 Projected $6,315,053; none in the Actual years) and shown only where there is a draw;
  - the total itself, **exactly as the city printed it** whenever the book prints a total for exactly the lines in view (a fund total, a department roll-up or a bureau's section total). The site re-adds the lines only to check it: rounding (up to $5 on a section, up to 50 cents a line on a roll-up) is not mentioned, and a real gap keeps the printed figure, adds a ledger-red **≠** and a note with the lines' sum, the difference and the reviewed explanation from `data/known_issues.csv`. Where the book prints no such total (all funds together, departments and bureaus regrouped to the 2027 org chart, categories, a line-item search), the figure is the site's own sum, labelled **calculated**. The same rule applies to the fund table, the department ledger, bureau cards, Totals by Year, line-item footers and What changed; About explains it once;
  - on the Income view, a **tax tile** for each of the year's tax changes beside the Income change tile, in the same shape: the change in the rate with its arrow ("+0.20 pts (+18.2%)", "+0.290 mills (+10.0%)", "No change") and the rate before → after ("2026 1.10% → 2027 Revised 1.30%"). City: `data/policy_notes.csv` rows for that year and figure with `rate_from`/`rate_to` or `change_pct`; County: the rates from `data/county/context_facts.csv` for the context year. General Fund and All funds only. They appear in the home "What changed in 2027" panel and on the What changed page. For an earlier year (no panel) and on department and bureau pages, short highlights sit under the total instead: the tax changes and the three income lines that changed most against the comparison, each linked to its page. Income views never mention money drawn from savings: the draw is described with spending (the deck, What changed's spending sentence and tile) and on the Reserve Fund page;
  - the controls on wide screens: the status stamp for the figure in view (for example "2027: Revised proposal, not adopted"), then Budget year, Compare with, Fund and Spending / Income in one row; on phones a compact bar naming only the selected view;
  - side by side (stacked on narrower screens): a **"What changed in 2027"** summary (the headline sentence, the four headline numbers and a "See everything that changed" button) and a **"Totals by Year"** line chart. The summary appears for the newest year: on the Spending view it leads with spending and positions, on the Income view with income and the reserve draw (and its button opens What changed at "How it's paid for");
  - a **Departments** ledger: one ruled row per department with the comparison figure, the selected figure, the signed change and its share of the total (the whole row is a link), with a pie of each department's share to its right. A chevron before each name unfolds that department's history under its row: the same chart and "Show values" table as the department page's **Over time** tab, every printed total linked to its page; one open at a time;
  - a **Staff** panel (City only, Spending view): a line chart of budgeted positions by year for the city (or the selected fund), 2022–2026 as the line and 2027 Proposed and Revised as marks, with a "Show values" table; then a ledger of budgeted positions by department in the Income Sources format (departments as on the 2027 org chart): Department | comparison | selected | signed change with % | share, following the **Budget year** and **Compare with** controls, with an **All** row and a pie of each department's share of the selected figure to its right (Share of 2027 Revised positions by department). Positions come from the staffing schedules, which print budgeted positions only: 2027 Revised and Proposed are their own schedules, and any other figure (Actual, Projected, Amended) is that year's budgeted positions (2026 Projected → the 2026 budgeted schedule); a year with no schedule (2020, 2021, the 2028 and 2029 projections) shows "—" with a note, no change, and as the selected figure no pie. Each department's name folds the full year-by-year chart and values down under it, like a line item's history (one at a time; selecting a pie slice opens or closes that row). Positions whose account number in that year's book matches no office on the 2027 org chart are counted on a "Not placed in a department" row, so the All row is every position in the schedules. The totals are the site's sums of the schedules' lines (the 2026 book's schedule lacks the Public Works Director page; see Known problems);
  - an **Income Sources** panel (Income view only, City and County, following the Fund selector; not on the Reserve Fund): income by source in the line items' format, following the **Budget year** and **Compare with** controls: Source | comparison figure (omitted with "No comparison") | selected figure | signed change with % | share of the selected figure (the bar is left out beside the pie), and an **All** row equal to the page's income total in both figures. Sources with nothing in either figure are left out; rows sort by the selected figure, largest first. Taxes are shown one by one (real estate, earned income, local services, real estate transfer, payments in lieu of taxes, tax penalties, interest and refunds), with grants and reimbursements and the one-time federal ARPA revenue replacement, from [`data/income_sources.csv`](../data/income_sources.csv); "Charges to other funds" is what one fund charges another (left out of All funds); every other line uses the group printed by the newest book that has it, so renamed groups such as "Public Safety Revenues" → "Public Safety - Police Fees" stay one row. Draws from savings and transfers between funds never appear. A source's name folds down its full history under it, like a line item's: legend, line chart of every year (Actual, Projected, Proposed, Revised, then the city's projections) and Show values (Year, Figure, Amount, each linked to its page), one at a time. The County has no source rows, so its panel shows the printed groups; a pie to the right of the table shows each source's share of the selected figure (Share of 2027 Revised income by source), keyed by the rows like the other pies; selecting a slice opens or closes that source's history row (the grey All other slice does nothing);
  - an "Income vs. Spending by Fund" chart and table, with a pie of each fund's share of spending (income on the Income view) to the right of the table.
  - Each of these pies (and the Staff ledgers' pies, on home and on department pages) uses the rows of its list as its key: every row starts with its slice's color swatch, in the list's order, and rows past the seventh share one grey "All other" slice. Selecting a slice opens that row's page (Income Sources and the Staff ledgers have no pages: their slice opens or closes that row's history). Where the list and the pie don't fit side by side (narrower screens, or the rail beside a mid-size window), the pie sits above the list.
- **The Home Rule 6% tax revenue limit** (City only; `budget.json` `tax_limit`, built by `scripts/tax_limit.py` from `config/tax_limit.json` and `data/tax_limit_lines.csv`). Charter § 8.08 caps the rise in General Fund tax revenue at 6% over the year before unless Council waives it by a majority of the whole Council plus one. The build tests every budget made under the charter (books from 2025 on): the sum of the four taxes (real estate, real estate transfer, earned income, local services) in the budget's column against the same lines in the same book's Projected column for the year before (its revised projection where it prints one). Over the limit, a flag "▲ Exceeds the 6% tax revenue limit" with "Needs a Council waiver vote, majority of the whole Council plus one (§ 8.08)", the change, and for 2025–2027 a note that § 13.14 may waive the limit in the charter's first two years; within it, one muted line ("2026 budget: within the 6% tax revenue limit, +5.1% from 2025 Projected"). It shows under the tiles in the home "What changed in 2027" panel and on What changed (General Fund or All funds; it follows the figure chosen in Budget year, 2027 Revised or 2027 Proposed), and under the total on the Income view for an earlier year (nothing before 2025). Each links to the test table on How the City budget is made. `check_dataset.py` recomputes every test from the CSVs.
- **What counts as income.** Income is money coming in. Revenue lines that are not, listed in [`data/income_rules.csv`](../data/income_rules.csv) and carried into `budget.json` as `not_income` ({account index: class}), are handled by class everywhere (totals, the Income vs. Spending table, department and bureau pages, Totals by Year, "stands out" lines and What changed):
  - `draw` (money from savings, the "Fund Balance Reserve" lines) and `transfer` (money moved in from another of the government's own funds: the City's "Tsfr'd From Utility Funds", the County's "Transfer From Other Funds" and "County Cash") are never income. The draw is described only with spending (the Spending deck, What changed's spending sentence, tile and revision list, the projection note) and on the Reserve Fund page, as money used to balance the budget; no Income view mentions it.
  - `charge` (what one fund charges another for services: the City's indirect-cost, Treasury and IT allocations, vehicle upkeep and grounds billing; the County's "Indirect Revenue" and other "Charges to County Agencies") is income of the fund that is paid, tagged "Charged to another of the City's funds" on its line, and left out of the All funds total, where the paying fund's income already counts it.
  - `income` marks a reviewed look-alike that is money from outside (a federal indirect-cost grant, the state's liquid fuels allocation).
  - Because income leaves these lines out, an income total is usually the site's own sum, labelled **calculated**, rather than the printed revenue total. The Income vs. Spending footnote says the All funds total leaves out charges between funds. Spending is shown as printed, transfers out included. `check_dataset.py` (both entities) fails on any revenue line whose name looks like a draw, transfer or charge (or any County "Charges to County Agencies" line) without a rule.
- **Departments and bureaus follow the city's 2027 org chart** (`Budget PDFs - City/2027 Org Chart.pdf`). Every budget line is placed in an org-chart bureau by its account number (org code), so every year is grouped the same way, even where an older book printed a group under another department or name. For example, Human Resources appears under Executive in every year. The full mapping, with the evidence for each choice, is in [`data/org_structure_2027.csv`](../data/org_structure_2027.csv).
- **Department page** (`#/department/public-safety`). Top to bottom:
  - breadcrumbs (Home › Department › Bureau), the total and status, and a row of in-page links (What changed · Compare · Bureaus · Staff · Categories · Line items);
  - **"What does this department do?"**: a collapsible description quoted from the city's `Org Descriptions 2027.pdf`, with a small link to its page in that PDF (closed by default; Legislative, City Controller, Public Safety and General Revenue have none, so it is left out);
  - the controls (the same row as home, compact on phones);
  - **What changed**: the headline sentence (the budget change, amount and percent), then a short summary generated from the data: **budgeted positions** in the prior year's schedule against the 2027 Revised schedule with the net change (how many moved between funds with their work or sit on an office one book prints no schedule page for, and the titles whose count changed where the two books' titles can be matched); the **largest increases or decreases** (up to three line items, or the categories when no few lines explain half the change), each amount linked to its page; and money **moved into or out of the General Fund**. Then the fund moves and, behind short disclosures, the change by bureau, positions and the largest line changes. Bureau pages get the same;
  - **Compare**, one panel with two tabs: "This year vs. comparison" (both totals, the change, the by-category bars and a category table behind "Show as a table") and "Over time" (totals by year; in 2027 Revised is a solid green dot joined by a dashed line and Proposed a hollow diamond; the axis starts above zero and says so);
  - a **bureau list**: one card per bureau or office on the org chart (name, share of the department, selected-vs-comparison bars, the whole card is a link; two columns on wide screens), with a pie of each card's share to the right, keyed the same way as the home page pies, with several budget groups combined where the chart has one bureau. Budget groups that are not a bureau (for example Administrative Services' city-wide Insurance, Fringe Benefits, Debt Service and Community Involvement budgets, or a department's fee revenue) form one card labelled "Department-wide costs (not a bureau)" or "Department revenue (not a bureau)". Departments with no bureaus on the chart (Legislative, City Controller, and General Revenue, which is not on the chart) have no bureau list and no bureau pages;
  - **Staff**: a line chart of the page's budgeted positions by year (same marks as Totals by Year: the line for 2022–2026, a hollow diamond for 2027 Proposed, a solid dot for 2027 Revised, with a "Show values" table and a note when the revision changed the count), then two ledgers by bureau in the Income Sources format: Bureau | comparison | selected | signed change with % | share (the same **Budget year** and **Compare with** controls; the All row is the sum of the bureau rows), each with a pie of the bureaus' shares of the selected figure to its right, keyed by the rows (on a bureau page, that bureau alone: one row of comparison, selected and change, no pie and no toggle, the chart above being its history). On a department page each bureau's name in either ledger is a toggle: in the positions ledger it folds that bureau's positions chart and values down under its name, in the salaries ledger its Salaries and wages by year (the same chart and values as a line item's history, projections included). One history is open in the section at a time, and selecting a pie slice opens or closes that row; the bureau cards link to the bureau pages:
    - **Budgeted positions**, from each year's staffing schedule (2022–2027, 2027 as Proposed and Revised). The city publishes budgeted positions only, never actual headcount: 2027 Revised and Proposed show their own schedules, and an Actual, Projected or Amended figure for a year shows that year's budgeted positions; a year with no schedule shows "—" and the pie needs the selected figure to have one. Counts are as printed (fractions are shared positions); full-time and part-time are not split, because the 2023–2025 books don't mark them. Each count opens its schedule page;
    - **Salaries and wages**: the "Salaries & Wages" budget lines (salaries, overtime, temporary wages; benefits are separate), using the series chosen in the controls directly (Actual 2020–2025, Projected 2026, Proposed and Revised 2027, and the city's 2028 and 2029 projections as a comparison), with every year in the fold-down history;
    - on a bureau page, a list of every position title in the newest schedule with its count and salary;
  - a **category table**: each spending category with its amount and a share bar;
  - a searchable, sortable line-item table (20 rows at a time) with the account code under each name and **Export this view (CSV)**, which downloads every row matching the search, with figure, comparison, change and the source book and page, built in the browser;
  - **a line's history:** each line's name is a quiet toggle (▸ closed, ▾ open, like "Show values"). It opens a full-width row directly under the line, one at a time, with a small chart of that line across every year, the "stands out" note and any note the book prints about it, and a **Show values** table (Year, Figure, Amount). Each year shows one kind of figure by the site's usual rules (Actual, then Projected, then Proposed and Revised, then the city's projections; the County's budgets), taken from the same data as the table, and every amount links to its budget-book page; a year with no row for the line shows a dash. Re-sorting, searching or "Show more" closes it.
- **Bureau page** (`#/department/<dept>/bureau/<bureau>`, e.g. `#/department/administrative-services/bureau/bureau-of-treasury`): the same layout for one org-chart bureau, across all its funds and years (including its own compact **What changed** section with positions), with **"What does this bureau do?"** from the descriptions PDF. Public Works bureaus also name their division. The department-wide group has a page too (`.../bureau/department-wide`) that lists the budget groups in it. Older bureau links (`.../bureau/<fund>--<printed group>`) redirect to the bureau that group now belongs to, or to the department page.
- **Reserve Fund** (in the Fund selector): the General Fund balance, from each book's "Fund Balance Projection" page. It shows the year-end balance and how it changes each year, using the same one-figure-per-year rule. Where the city's page disagrees with itself, the Revised column is used and the difference is explained on the page: for 2025 the 2027 book prints $13,479,257 (Proposed column) and $25,604,677 (Revised column), so 2025 shows +111.2%, flagged on the chart bar and the table row with a link to the known issues and the source page. Under the "How the Balance Changes" table, a note explains why the General Fund pages show more income than the Revenues line just above it: the budget counts the money drawn from savings as income (its "Fund Balance Reserve" lines) and the projection does not ("The General Fund pages show $87,718,539 of income for 2027 Revised, not the $82,983,451 on the Revenues line above. The difference is the $4.7M drawn from savings ($1.9M of it from dedicated data center revenue) …"). It appears only for years whose projection prints a Revenues line (2026 Projected, 2027 Proposed and Revised). For 2026 Projected and 2027 Revised the draw equals the page's drawdown; for 2027 Proposed the city's own page does not add up (a $475,000 gap, logged in the known issues), and `check_dataset.py` reports it rather than forcing it.
- **Jump to… search** (header): finds departments, bureaus and line items (by name or account code). A line item opens its bureau page with the line-item search filled in. Keyboard and screen-reader accessible (combobox).
- **What changed** (`#/changes`; reached from the summary at the top of the home page and from every department page): what is different in the newest budget. It follows the Fund selector (General Fund by default; the Reserve Fund too, which shows how the General Fund balance changes: the year-end balance in the newest figure against the comparison's, the Fund Balance Projection lines that move it side by side with their effect on the balance, and the draw as "$4.7M drawn from savings ($1.9M of it from dedicated data center revenue)"; where the comparison year prints only a closing balance, as the Actual years do, it says so) and the figure chosen in Budget year (2027 Revised or 2027 Proposed), from every way in: the contents rail link carries the fund, figure, comparison and Spending / Income of the page you are on (from an earlier year it opens the newest year). A fund with no lines in either budget being compared (Liquid Fuels, Long's Park) gets a sentence saying so, with links to the funds that have them; arriving from the Reserve Fund shows the General Fund and says why. "Show first" (Spending / Income) puts that side's sections first. It and compares with the prior year's **Projected** figure by default. A badge says when a proposal is being compared with a year-end estimate or with actual spending, with a one-click switch to the latest **Actual**. The page shows:
  - a one-sentence summary generated from the data (for example: "The 2027 Revised budget spends $87.7M from the General Fund, $5.0M (6.0%) more than the city expects to spend in 2026. $1.25M of that is Street Cleaning and Trees work moved from the Stormwater Management Fund. It's balanced by $4.7M drawn from savings ($1.9M of it from dedicated data center revenue).");
  - four headline numbers: the spending change (on the Income view, the income change), the reserve draw, the change in full-time positions with the number budgeted (its "How this is counted" link opens the Positions section at its explanation, also from the home page), and how the revision changed the proposal;
  - **Moved into / out of the General Fund**: work that moved between funds, found from the city's own line notes ("Budget has been moved to the General Fund …"), with the dollars and positions on each side and the notes quoted. Because the General Fund is paid for mostly by taxes and the other funds by fees, every move into or out of it is called out. In 2027 these are Street Cleaning and Trees (Stormwater → General Fund), Water grounds maintenance (Water → General Fund, billed back to the Water Fund), and nuisance abatement (General Fund → Solid Waste);
  - changes by category and by department (bars, or a table);
  - **Positions**: first the budgeted count for the fund ("Full-time positions budgeted, General Fund: 445.65 in the 2027 Revised schedule, against 427.75 in the 2026 budget book", each count linked to its schedule page, with how many moved in or out with their work and the part-time count), then how positions are counted (each line of a book's position schedule is one position or the share of one paid from that office, full-time and part-time as the schedule marks them, positions paid for rather than people employed), then position counts per org-chart bureau, the "Schedule change" (the two books' schedules compared line for line) and the "Real change". The real change leaves out what is not a real change, and each item is listed with a note: positions that moved between funds with their work; council seats marked full-time in one book and part-time in the next; bureaus a book has no staffing page for (from `known_issues.csv`); a bureau printed under a new org code; and titles renamed between books. Benefits are shown in dollars. Salary growth is not shown here;
  - the largest line-item increases and decreases, and new and removed lines;
  - **How it's paid for**: income changes by source (the reserve draw is not income and is not listed here). The year's tax changes are tiles under the Income summary sentence (City policy notes; County rate change);
  - **A line that stands out:** where a line in one of the two budgets being compared is far above the years either side of it (see "Lines that stand out" below), a note names it with its amounts and says what the change would be without it. Comparing 2027 Revised with 2025 Actual: "Leaving that line out, income rises $5.7M rather than falling $1.0M."
  - **What the revision changed** (Proposed → Revised), with the tax-policy notes from `data/policy_notes.csv` beside the revenue line each one affects. Tax rates are not in the budget tables, so these notes are entered by hand, each with its source.
  - Each department and bureau page has a short "What changed" section (headline, a two-to-four-item summary of positions, the largest changes and General Fund moves, then bureaus, positions, largest lines and moves between funds) linking to the full page.
  - The figures come from `web/data/changes.json`, built by `scripts/changes.py` (called by `build_dataset.py`). `check_dataset.py` ties every total in it to the series data, lists each fund shift and position adjustment, and re-derives every number in the department and bureau summaries (`summary` in changes.json) from the series and the staffing CSVs.
- **Lines that stand out:** under **Totals by Year** (home, department and bureau pages), a note names any line whose amount in one year is at least twice its amount in both the year before and the year after (both printed and above zero) and whose jump is at least 5% of its fund's total that year (`SPIKE_X`, `SPIKE_SHARE` in `app.js` `lineSpikes()`; `check_dataset.py` lists the same lines with the same rule). It is found from the lines, never typed in. Today that is four lines: the 2025 General Fund **Building Permits** ($9,032,578 in 2025 Actual, 2027 book p. 42, against $1,339,520 in 2024 and $1,936,328 projected for 2026), the 2021 Sewer and Water OPEB contributions, and 2023 Stormwater professional services. Where `data/line_notes.csv` records what the book says about that line, the note adds it with a link: for Building Permits, that the city used data center building permit revenue to pay $4,162,525 of General Fund debt in 2026 (2027 book p. 7); the book does not break the 2025 figure down.
- **Funds and terms** (`#/terms`, `#/county/terms`, in the header): every fund and whether it is paid for by taxes, fees, grants or other money, the Reserve Fund / General Fund Balance, and every figure word (Actual, Projected, Proposed, Revised, Adopted, Amended, Projection, Budget, calculated) and what is not shown (year-to-date columns, older books' own proposals). The "What do these mean?" panel under the controls, the status stamp and About link to it.
- **How the City budget is made** (`#/budget-calendar`, in the header after Funds and terms, City only; linked from About): the City's budget calendar under its Home Rule Charter (in effect since January 1, 2025). Nothing on it is read from the budget data; the wording is in `app.js` (`calendarPage()`, `calSteps()`) and the two source PDFs it links to are named in `web/config.json` `entities.city.budget_calendar` (the City's charter PDF, and its 2024 audited financial statements for the rules before 2025). Every statement cites its charter section; the section number is the link and opens the charter PDF at the page that prints it (`CHARTER_PAGE` in `app.js`; the PDF's page numbers equal the charter's printed "Page | N"). Top to bottom:
  - **The budget calendar**: the fiscal year is the calendar year (§ 8.01), then a timeline of ten steps in time order, from the capital program (by June 30) to a revised budget after a municipal election year (by February 15). Each step has its month on an ink band (shown when the month changes; steps the charter ties to another step rather than a date have no month), its timing, a serif title, who acts (Mayor / Council / Public tags), what happens, and, where the charter says, what happens if the date is missed. A date the charter sets carries a green rule and a green "Deadline" label with its section. On wide columns (880px and up, a container query) the band runs across, five steps a row, two rows with an arrowhead at each row's end; narrower (phones, tablets, the 1100px rail layout's narrow column) it runs down the left. The timeline is one ordered list, so screen readers read it in time order; the band and markers are decoration. A key under it explains the Deadline label, the who-tags and the steps with no month;
  - **The Mayor's budget**: the October submission (§ 8.02), what the budget message must explain (§ 8.03) and what the budget must show (§ 8.04), with a note that § 8.04(f)'s two years of estimates are why the City publishes the projections the site shows as Projection;
  - **The 6% limit on tax revenue** (§ 8.08): what counts, how a waiver is proposed and passed (§ 8.02(c)–(d), majority of the whole Council plus one), that revenue above the limit is left out of the next year's limit, a worked example with made-up round numbers labelled as such, and § 13.14 quoted (the charter does not name the budget years it covers, and the page does not say). Under it, **The City's budgets against the limit** (`id="cal-limit-test"`): a table of each budget made under the charter (2025 budget, 2026 budget, 2027 Proposed, 2027 Revised) with its General Fund tax revenue, the year before (the same book's Projected figure), the change and "▲ Exceeds: needs a waiver" or "Within", from `budget.json` `tax_limit` (sums labelled calculated, each linked to the book page that prints the lines), and "Which lines count" from `data/tax_limit_lines.csv`;
  - **Council's deadlines** (§ 8.05, § 8.10): hearings, the published answers, amendments and the 10% / 25% rule, adoption by December 15 (or the Mayor's budget becomes official), the Mayor's veto and Council's override by December 31, and the capital program by September 30;
  - **When you can take part**: the public moments the charter sets (reading the budget, hearings, the question period, the answers, the re-publication of big amendments, the waiver vote, the capital program hearing, the monthly reports and quarterly reviews);
  - **During the budget year**: monthly reports and quarterly reviews (§ 8.11), changes after adoption (§ 8.07), and the § 8.06 revised budget after a municipal election (with a note that it is not the site's Revised figure);
  - **Before 2025**: when voters approved the charter (its cover) and when it took effect (§ 13.02), the earlier rules from the City's 2024 audited financial statements (Optional Third Class City Charter Law; budget to Council before November 30, approved by the end of December), and that the site's 2022–2024 books were made under them.
  - On the County (`#/county/budget-calendar`) the nav link is hidden and the address shows one sentence saying the page covers the City, with a link to it. The County's budget calendar has not been researched.
- **Page headings** name the budget: a running head ("City of Lancaster", "County of Lancaster", from `web/config.json`) above every heading, and the same name first in the browser tab title.
- **About the data** (`#/about`, linked in the header):
  - which book supplies each year, including the projections book and its assumptions;
  - how the figures were validated;
  - every known error in the city's source books, each reviewed by a person;
  - download links;
  - (City) a link to How the City budget is made.

City funds: General, Stormwater Management, Sewer, Water, Solid Waste & Recycling, Liquid Fuels, Long's Park, plus the Reserve Fund view. County funds: General Fund and the lettered funds B, E, EI, F, G, H, R and Y (named in `web/config.json` `fund_names`), plus the General Fund Balance view. Both open on the **General Fund**.

## 3. Controls and addresses

**Header:** Overview, What changed, Reserve Fund (the County's General Fund Balance), Funds and terms, Budget calendar (How the City budget is made; City only) and About the data, plus **Jump to…** search and the light/dark switch. On wide screens a contents rail shows where you are and an "On this page" list; phones get an "On this page" outline instead.

**Controls** (the same component on every page that has them: home with any fund, All funds and the Reserve Fund, department and bureau pages, and What changed) are **always open on wide screens**, with nothing behind a button; on phones they start as a compact bar naming only the current view (for example "2027 Revised · 2026 Projected · General Fund · Spending"), placed above the headline, which expands when tapped and closes again when the page scrolls. First the status stamp for the figure in view, then one group named "View" (a `<fieldset>` with a hidden legend) holding, in Tab order: **Budget year**, **Compare with**, **Fund**, then the **Spending / Income** tabs ("Show first" on What changed). Each select has a visible `<label for>`. On desktop they sit in one row, ended by the "What do these mean?" toggle (from 1024px; narrower, the toggle sits on its own line under them). The whole bar (stamp, controls, toggle) **stays pinned to the top of the main column while the page scrolls**, on opaque paper with a 1px ink rule under it once pinned; it never covers the contents rail. In-page jumps ("On this page", the rail outline) and keyboard focus land below it: `pinControls()` in `app.js` measures the bar into the CSS variable `--pin-h`, which `html { scroll-padding-top }` uses. On phones (767px and narrower) the pinned bar is a 45px button (`.controls-compact`, `aria-expanded`): only the selected values show, the status stamp and fields are hidden until it is tapped, and scrolling closes them again; a field's note ("Only the funds this department uses are listed.") is read by screen readers but not shown. Printing, the bar is not pinned.

**"What do these mean?"** is one explainer for both the figures and the funds. Its toggle (a button with `aria-expanded` and `aria-controls`) opens a panel the full width of the bar, laid over the page under it (it never pushes the page down), with two labelled groups: **Figures** (City: Actual, Projected, Proposed, Revised, Amended, Adopted, Projection, calculated; County: Budget, Actual, calculated) and **Funds** (what a fund is, then each fund in the page's Fund list, with the Reserve Fund / General Fund Balance). The definitions sit in columns that reflow to the width (three on a desktop, one on a phone). The figure in Budget year is marked "shown", the one in Compare with "comparing" and the chosen fund "selected" (a word, bold and a green rule, not colour alone). The panel ends with "More in Funds and terms →". Its height is capped (60% of the window) and it scrolls inside; the toggle, Escape (focus returns to the toggle), a click outside the controls or tabbing out of them close it. Its wording is the Funds and terms page's own (`figureWords()`, `FUND_INTRO`, `FUND_INFO`, `balanceWord()` in `app.js`), so the two never disagree. Changing a control re-draws the page without moving focus (it stays on that control, or on the chosen tab), and a polite live region (`#view-status` in `index.html`) says the new view in one sentence: "Showing 2027 Revised compared with 2026 Projected, General Fund, spending." The Spending / Income tabs are a radio group: only the chosen one is in the Tab order, and the arrow keys (and Home / End) move the choice.
- **Budget year:** every year and figure the site can show, newest first, grouped by year (`<optgroup label="2027">`, each option named in full): City "2027 Revised", "2027 Proposed", "2026 Projected", "2026 Amended", "2025 Actual", "2025 Amended", … "2021 Actual", "2021 Amended", "2020 Actual"; County "2026 Budget" … "2023 Budget". Picking one sets Compare with to the usual default for it (the year before, like for like where it exists, else that year's main figure) unless the visitor had chosen a comparison, which stays. On What changed it lists only the newest year's figures (2027 Revised, 2027 Proposed), because that page is always about the newest year. On the Reserve Fund it lists the balances (2027 Revised, 2027 Proposed, 2026 Projected, 2025 Actual (unaudited), …) and there is no Compare with (each balance is shown with its change from the year before); on the County's General Fund Balance it lists the years.
- **Compare with:** the same list, plus "No comparison". The figure chosen as Budget year stays in the list but cannot be picked ("2027 Revised (the budget year shown)"). On What changed it lists the comparisons `changes.json` holds for the chosen figure.
- **Fund:** a single fund, "All funds", or the Reserve Fund. Every Fund list is in the same order: All funds, General Fund, the other funds, then the Reserve Fund last. Home, the Reserve Fund page and What changed offer the Reserve Fund; department pages offer only the funds that department has lines in (the reserve has no departments), with a note under the list saying so; bureau pages have no Fund list (they cover all of a bureau's funds).
- **Show:** Spending / Income, only where the page has both.

**On the pages:**
- Click a **department row** to open its page. Use the **Department** dropdown on that page to switch departments, and the bureau cards to drill into a bureau.
- **Line items:**
  - the count reads like "97 line items (1 only in 2026 Projected)": every line in the table, and how many exist only in the comparison figure; the header counts the same way;
  - on phones each line's change shows under its amount;
  - type in the search box to filter by name, account number, bureau, fund or category;
  - click a column header to sort;
  - use "Show more" to load more rows.
- Under the "Totals by Year" chart, **Show values** opens a table of the numbers behind it.
- **Shareable links:** every view's settings are kept in the address, so a link reopens exactly that view. For example, `#/department/public-safety?y=2027&m=proposed&c=2026-projected`. The Budget year select writes `y` and `m` together (`?y=2026&m=amended`), so addresses from before the selects keep working unchanged.

| Parameter | Meaning | Values |
|---|---|---|
| `y` | year | e.g. `2027` |
| `m` | figure | `actual`, `projected`, `proposed`, `revised`, `amended` (or `adopted` for a year whose books print no amended figure; an address naming the other one opens the one the year has) |
| `c` | compare with | `<year>-<figure>`, e.g. `2026-projected`, `2025-amended`, or `none` |
| `k` | show revenue | `revenue` (expenses is the default) |
| `f` | fund | a fund name, `Reserve Fund` (County: `General Fund Balance`), or `all` (General Fund is the default) |
