# Lancaster Budget Explorer

**https://budget.nazwel.net**

A free, independent way to read two local budgets: the **City of Lancaster, PA** and the **County of Lancaster, PA**. Every figure comes from the governments' own published documents, and every amount links to the page it came from, so you can check it yourself.

This is an independent project. It is not associated with the City or the County, and neither government has reviewed it.

**Questions or corrections:** budget@nazwel.net

---

## What the site is

The City and the County each publish their budget as long PDF books full of tables. This site reads those books, checks the arithmetic, and lays the numbers out so you can answer plain questions:

- Is the government spending more or less than last year, and by how much?
- Where does the money go, department by department, down to single line items?
- What changed in the newest budget, and why?
- How much is being drawn from savings to balance the budget?
- Which funds are paid for by taxes, and which by fees or grants?

Use the **City** and **County** tabs at the top of the page to switch between the two. Every page names the budget it shows above its heading ("City of Lancaster" or "County of Lancaster").

## Where the numbers come from

### City of Lancaster

The figures come from the City's budget books for 2022 through 2027, and a separate book of projections for 2028 and 2029. Each book prints several years side by side. To keep things from getting mixed up, the site shows **one main kind of figure for each year** (this is what the charts over time draw):

| Year | What you see | What it means |
|---|---|---|
| 2020–2025 | **Actual** | What was really spent or received, as reported in the budget book two years later. |
| 2026 | **Projected** | The City's estimate of how the year will end. It is not over yet, so there is no actual. |
| 2027 | **Proposed** and **Revised** | The mayor's first proposal and the revised proposal that replaced it. You can switch between them. Neither has been adopted. |
| 2028–2029 | **Projection** | The City's own estimate of the years after 2027. Shown lighter, only at the end of the charts and tables over time. |

You can also pick each year's **budget as passed**: the **Amended** budget (the adopted budget with the changes made during the year) where the next year's book prints one, otherwise the **Adopted** budget, never both. Today 2021 to 2026 all have an Amended figure. It is a plan, not what was spent: compare "2025 Actual" with "2025 Amended" to see how a year turned out against its budget. It is not drawn on the charts over time. The **Funds and terms** page explains every one of these words.

When the City publishes a new budget book, every year moves forward: the new year becomes Proposed and Revised, the year before becomes Projected, and older years become Actual.

### County of Lancaster

- **Budget, 2023–2026,** from the County's budget books for 2024, 2025 and 2026. Each book also reprints the year before. The 2026 budget was adopted on December 24, 2025 (Resolution 79 of 2025). The County's books print budgets only, so every County figure is labelled **Budget**.
- **Actual, 2023–2025,** from the County's audited annual financial reports. These show what each fund really took in and spent, but only as fund totals, General Fund income by source and spending by function, not line by line.
- **General Fund Balance,** from the budget resolution adopted with each year's budget (2024, 2025 and 2026), next to the audited balance at the end of each year.
- **The 2026 Budget in Context** (tax rate, assessed value, bond rating, staffing count, capital projects and agency grants) comes from the resolutions, budget books and audited reports. Only the staffing count rests on the County's 2026 budget presentation alone, and the page says so.

## How to use the site

**Overview.** The home page opens on the **General Fund**, the fund paid for mostly by taxes. At the top is the total and one sentence that says whether it is up or down and by how much. On Spending it also says how much is drawn from savings to balance the budget; Below it: a short "What changed" summary (on Income, with a tile for each tax change: the change in the rate, with its arrow, and the rate before and after), a chart of totals by year, every department with its share, and income against spending for each fund.

**Changing what you see.** Every page has the same controls above the figures: on a phone they start as a one-line bar showing just the selected view (tap to open the fields, scroll to close them); on wider screens they are always open, under a stamp that names the figure in view (such as "2027: Revised proposal, not adopted"):

- **Budget year,** the year and figure to show, such as "2027 Revised", "2027 Proposed", "2026 Projected", "2026 Amended" or "2025 Actual";
- **Compare with,** any other year and figure from the same list, or no comparison;
- **Fund,** one fund, all funds together, or the savings balance (Reserve Fund or General Fund Balance);
- **Show,** Spending or Income.

They work with a keyboard (Tab moves between them; arrow keys change the Spending / Income choice) and with screen readers, which announce the new view after each change. They stay at the top of the page while you scroll (on a phone in a compact two-row form), so you can change the view from anywhere on the page. "What do these mean?", the last of the controls, opens the definitions of the figures (Actual, Projected, Revised and the rest) and of the funds in the Fund list, with the ones you are looking at marked, and links to the full Funds and terms page.

**Departments and bureaus.** Click a department to open its page: what the department does (quoted from the City's own descriptions), what changed, a comparison with another year, its bureaus, its staff (City only), its spending categories and every line item. City departments and bureaus follow the City's 2027 organization chart in every year, so you can follow one office over time. County departments are grouped as the County's books group them (General Government, Courts and Row Offices, Corrections, Human Services); offices headed by an elected official are marked "Elected office".

**Staff** (City only). The home page and each department page show budgeted positions, and department pages also salaries and wages, by department or bureau for the year you pick against the comparison, with a pie of each one's share. Click a name to see its positions (or salaries) year by year. These are budgeted positions from the staffing schedules, not a count of people employed.

**Income sources.** On the Income view, the home page breaks income down by source: each tax on its own (real estate, earned income, local services, real estate transfer and the rest), grants and reimbursements, fees and the other groups the books print, with a pie of each source's share. Click a source to see it year by year, 2020 to the city's 2029 projection.

**Line items.** Search by name or account number, sort by any column, click a line's name to see that line across the years (with a link to the book page for each figure), and press **Export this view (CSV)** to download every matching line with its source page.

**What changed** (in the header) compares any two budgets: pick one in Budget year and any other in Compare with. It opens on the newest budget against the year before, and shows the change in spending and income, the largest line-item changes, money and staff **moved into or out of the General Fund** (the City's fees-paid funds and the tax-paid General Fund are kept apart, so every move between them is called out), budgeted positions (for any two budgets that have a position schedule, 2022 on), and what the revised proposal changed (moves between funds and the revision are shown for the newest budget's usual comparisons). Where one line is far above the years either side of it, the page names it and says what the change would be without it.

**What counts as income.** Income means money coming in: taxes, fees, fines, grants, state and federal payments, interest and rent. The budget books also list as revenue some money that is not coming in, and the site leaves it out of income:

- **money drawn from savings** to balance the budget (the City's "Fund Balance Reserve" lines);
- **transfers** from another of the government's own funds.

When one fund charges another for services (for example the Water Fund paying the General Fund for billing, IT and administration), the charge stays in the income of the fund that is paid and is marked on its line. The **All funds** total leaves it out, because the paying fund's income already counts that money. Spending is shown as printed, transfers to other funds included. On the Income view the site points out what changed, in the "What changed" summary on the home page and under the total on department pages: tax increases or cuts proposed for the year (for example the earned income tax going from 1.1% to 1.3% in the 2027 Revised proposal, or the County's 10% real estate tax increase in 2026) and the income lines that changed most against the year you compare with. How much is drawn from savings to balance the budget is shown with spending, not income. The Funds and terms page explains the rule, and [`data/income_rules.csv`](data/income_rules.csv) lists every line kept out, with the budget book's own explanation.

**Reserve Fund (City)** shows the General Fund's savings at the end of each year and what moves it. **General Fund Balance (County)** shows the County's balance two ways, side by side and never added together: the budget resolution's estimate, and the audited balance at December 31.

**2028–2029 projections (City).** The charts and tables over time continue past 2027 with a dotted gray line on a shaded band, with the City's assumptions underneath (for example no water, sewer, stormwater or trash rate increases and 3% salary increases). They are estimates, not budgets, and are never the headline figure.

**What would you pay?** ("Your taxes" in the header, and "Estimate what you would pay →" under the property tax and earned income tax changes on the Income view and the What changed page) is a calculator. Choose City, County or both, type in your assessed values (this year's, and the new value on your reassessment notice; "Find your assessed values" looks them up on the County's property site by address or parcel number, in a new tab) and, for the City's earned income tax, your wages and business profits. It shows how each tax changes from this year to next year under the City's 2027 Revised budget, ends with your total increase or decrease, and then a table of both years with each rate linked to where it comes from. Because every property was reassessed for 2027, the City's property tax rate is lower next year but applies to your new value, so your bill can go up or down. The County has not set its 2027 rate yet, so the County shows this year only. It is an estimate, not a tax bill, and leaves out School District taxes and homestead exclusions. What you type stays in your browser.

**Funds and terms** (in the header) lists every fund, what pays for it (taxes, fees, grants or state money), and what each figure word means.

**Jump to…** in the header finds any department, bureau or line item by name or account number.

**Sharing.** The address in your browser keeps the year, figure, comparison and fund you chose, so a link opens exactly the view you see.

**Budget calendar** (in the header, City only; the page is How the City budget is made) shows the City's budget calendar under its Home Rule Charter as a month-by-month timeline: when the Mayor must present the budget and what it must contain, the 6% limit on tax revenue and how it can be waived, Council's deadlines and what happens if one is missed, and when the public can take part. Every rule links to the page of the charter that sets it. It also tests each City budget made under the charter against the 6% limit: the four City taxes in the General Fund (real estate, real estate transfer, earned income and local services) against the same budget book's figure for the year before. The 2025 budget (+12.1%), the 2027 Proposed (+11.7%) and the 2027 Revised (+8.6%) are over it, which needs a Council waiver vote; the 2026 budget (+5.1%) is within it. These sums are the site's own, labelled calculated. The flag also shows on the Income view and on What changed. The County's budget calendar has not been researched.

**About the data** lists the books used for each year, how the figures were checked, every known error in the source books, and download links for the data.

## How to check a number

1. **Click the amount.** Every dollar amount is a link. It opens the source PDF at the page that prints it.
2. **Compare it with the page.** Totals are shown **exactly as the government printed them**. Where no printed total covers exactly what you are looking at (all funds together, a search result, a regrouped department), the site adds up the lines itself and labels the figure **calculated**.
3. **Look for ≠.** If the lines under a printed total do not add up to it beyond rounding, the site keeps the printed figure, marks it **≠**, and gives the lines' sum, the difference and an explanation.
4. **Read the known problems.** About the data lists every error found in the source books, each reviewed by a person.
5. **Download the data** from About the data, or directly: `budget.json`, `changes.json`, `positions.json` (position changes for any two budgets), `staffing.json` and `conflicts.json` at https://budget.nazwel.net/data/, and the County's under https://budget.nazwel.net/data/county/. The source PDFs are at https://budget.nazwel.net/pdfs/ and https://budget.nazwel.net/pdfs/county/.

## Known limits

**City**
- **2027 is not adopted.** The 2027 figures are the mayor's Proposed and Revised budgets. When the City's next book arrives, 2027 becomes that book's Projected year-end estimate, and its Amended (or Adopted) budget can be picked too.
- **Actual figures lag.** A year's actual spending is first printed in the budget book two years later, so 2026 is still an estimate.
- **Positions are budgeted positions,** not a headcount of people employed. Fractions are positions shared between offices. Salary growth is not shown; benefits changes are shown in dollars.
- **The books contain errors.** About the data lists 40 reviewed problems in the City's books. The biggest: the 2027 book prints two different General Fund balances for the end of 2025 ($13,479,257 and $25,604,677); the site uses the Revised one and flags the 2025 change. The 2027 Proposed balance page is also $475,000 off from its own figures.
- **The 2026 book is a scanned image.** It was read by text recognition, and every misread figure was corrected against the book's own totals, with the evidence recorded.
- **Some funds appear only in some years** (Liquid Fuels, Long's Park), because only some books print them.
- **Projections** for 2028 and 2029 are the City's estimates, with no year-end balance, so the Reserve Fund ends at 2027.

**County**
- **No line-by-line actuals.** The County's books print budgets only. Audited actual figures exist only for 2023–2025, and only at the fund level, by income source and by spending function. No variance between budget and actual is shown, because the two are counted differently.
- **No staffing schedule.** The County does not print one, so County pages have no Staff section.
- **Two kinds of fund balance.** The budget resolutions' balances are estimates and do not match the audited ones; the site shows both and never combines them. About the data lists 27 reviewed problems and differences in the County's documents.
- **Fund names.** The County's books print funds as letters (Fund B, Fund Y and so on); the names shown come from the County's audited reports.
- **The 2024 book is a scanned photocopy.** It was read by text recognition, and every misread figure was corrected and recorded with its evidence.
